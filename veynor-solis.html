01 — Hardware

Ours. Down to the board.

Built around the Rockchip RK3588 — eight cores and an onboard NPU, silicon usually reserved for flagship tablets, not phones — paired with an RK806 PMIC for rail distribution. We didn't license a reference design and skin it. We drew our own schematic, laid out our own 6-layer, 147×77mm carrier board, and built the handset PCB from a blank sheet.

02 — Power

Waste heat isn't wasted.

A thin-film solar band hides beneath the tinted rear ceramic, feeding a dedicated MPPT controller. Underneath the SoC, a grid of thermal vias carries heat to a TEG module — turning what would normally just dissipate into a trickle charge. The battery itself sits in a tool-free removable tray, charged over a magnetic pogo-pin port with no port to wear out or let water in.

03 — Software

Android underneath. Nothing like it on top.

Veynor Solis runs on Android 14 — the apps, the ecosystem, the compatibility you already rely on. But the launcher is entirely ours: a custom Jetpack Compose UI, built from scratch, with its own Camera, Gallery, Messages, Calendar, Notes, Music, Clock, and Settings. The Thermal screen already reads your device's real battery temperature — the solar/TEG readout next to it is the working preview of what the hardware feeds it.

04 — Longevity

Buy it once. Upgrade it forever.

The board is modular by design. A new revision drops in, the shell stays — upgradable at any time, for as long as we keep building. Veynor Solis is built to outlast the spec sheet it shipped with.
